Rio Grande flow explorer

Average streamflow in cubic feet per second (cfs) for each New Mexico segment of the Rio Grande, from the first gauge record in 1889 to Sep 20, 2026. A segment is the watershed (HUC8) its gauges sit in; each line is the mean of its gauges' weekly means, averaged again by month or year when you choose.

    How to read this

    • A segment value averages gauges at different points on the river. Diversions, returns and tributary inflow between gauges move it, so it is not the flow at any one place.
    • The gauges behind a segment change over the decades as gauges open and close. The hover readout shows how many were reporting; a jump in that count can move a line without any change in the river.
    • Each physical gauge is published by several agencies (USGS, USBR, Corps of Engineers, Colorado DWR). Copies are merged so a gauge counts once, and the USGS daily mean is used first.
    • Weekly: mean of daily means, Monday to Sunday, at least 4 days. Monthly: mean of the weeks starting in that month, at least 3. Yearly: mean of the weeks starting in that year, at least 45, so the current partial year is left out of yearly views. A gauge counts in a segment when it reported at least 26 weeks in the whole record.
    • Reservoirs changed the river over this record: Elephant Butte (1916), El Vado (1935), Caballo (1938), Abiquiu (1963), Cochiti (1975). Compare periods with that in mind.
    • Recent values are provisional and can be revised. The week in progress is not shown.
