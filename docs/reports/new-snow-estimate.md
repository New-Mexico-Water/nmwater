# How good is the new-snow estimate?

Generated by `scripts/new_snow_validation.py` on 2026-11-14; do not edit by hand. The rule is in `nmwater/derived/snotel_snow.py`.

## What is being checked

SNOTEL stations do not report snowfall. They report how deep the snow is and how much water it holds. The site estimates new snow from the two: a day counts when the depth rises 1 to 30 inches and the snow-water reading rises at least 0.1 inch the same day, and the day's new snow is the depth rise. The estimate is labelled an estimate everywhere on the site because snow settles, melts and blows, so it should read **below** what a person with a snowboard measured, and because the stations sit higher than most observers.

This report puts the estimate beside real snowfall measurements: volunteers (CoCoRaHS), National Weather Service cooperative observers (GHCN-Daily) and the agency stations that report snowfall (IEM). They are the only snowfall measurements in the archive.

## How the comparison is made

- **Pairs.** Every SNOTEL station in New Mexico with every snowfall observer within 12 km and 450 m of elevation. A pair counts when both have a reading on at least 200 days between November and April and the observer measured snow on at least 20 of them. 21 pairs qualify, at 9 stations and 20 observers.
- **Days.** Only days on which both have a value. A day with no reading is missing, never zero, on both sides.
- **Time of day.** A SNOTEL day *D* is the rise over the local day *D*: the archive's daily row dated *D* is the reading at the midnight that ends *D* (it equals the hourly reading at 07:00 UTC on *D*+1 exactly, in every month). A volunteer or cooperative observer reports in the morning for the 24 hours before, so the report dated *D*+1 (7 am on *D* to 7 am on *D*+1) is the match and overlaps the station's day by 17 hours. The timing section shows that this alignment fits the data best.
- **Elevation.** The observers sit lower than the stations (the gap is in the table). More snow falls higher up, so a station reading above its lower neighbour is expected, and the gap alone can make the ratio exceed one.

## Results

Summed over all 21 pairs and winters, the estimate is **68%** of what the observers measured (median pair 69%, middle half 57% to 80%).

On the days the observer measured an inch or more of snow (3788 such days), the estimate saw snow on 50% of them. On days the observer did not measure an inch, the estimate counted snow on 1730 days, 6% of them.

Day by day the two correlate at a median 0.57; added up over three days (which absorbs a day's offset in timing) 0.73.

## Why the rule has two conditions

The same stations and observers, three rules. The first counts every depth rise of the right size; the last uses only the water reading, scaled by ten inches of snow to an inch of water.

| Rule | Estimate / observer (all pairs) | Daily r (median) | Snow days seen | Days counted when the observer saw under an inch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| chosen rule | 68% | 0.57 | 50% | 1730 |
| depth rise only | 79% | 0.56 | 57% | 2727 |
| water rise only (x10 for snow:water) | 101% | 0.54 | 59% | 3159 |

Each rule trades snow days found against days counted that the observers did not see snow on. The chosen rule counts the fewest of those and has the best day-by-day agreement; it finds fewer of the observers' snow days than the other two. Depth alone, or water alone, finds more snow but also more that was not there. The water-only rule also carries no depth, so it could not feed the depth measure or the ski-area pages, and it needs an assumed snow density.

## What this means for the pages

- The estimate is a **floor** for new snow near the station, not a measurement; the pages say "estimate" beside every figure and link here.
- It compares places across years because the same rule and the same sensor apply every winter. It does not compare a station with a ski area's own report.
- A station reads only about two-thirds of what the observers measured and sees half of their snow days. Because the stations are higher, where more snow falls, the real shortfall is larger than that. A pair that reads far above one (Bateman, 195%) or far below (Taos 11.3 ESE, 20%, on 210 days) shows how much one observer's site and habits matter; the pages never present the estimate as an observer's figure.
- The newest day on the site is yesterday. Station days end at local midnight, so "new snow" is the last complete day, not the last 24 hours to now; the pages should say "yesterday".

## Limits

- Snowfall observers are in towns and valleys. Pairs at the same elevation as a SNOTEL station, in the same storm track, are few, and the volunteers who live there measure when it snows, so a day with no report is missing, not zero; those days are left out.
- The comparison does not test the estimate against what fell at the SNOTEL site itself: nobody measured that.
- GHCN-Daily snowfall is from cooperative observers who sometimes record a storm's total on the day they empty the gauge; the three-day correlation is less sensitive to that.
- Provisional data. SNOTEL values are not reviewed for the latest months.

## Reproduce

```
uv run python scripts/new_snow_validation.py
```

Needs a built catalog (`nmwater catalog build`). Parameters: 12 km, 450 m, at least 200 shared snow-season days and 20 observer snow days, SNOTEL data from 2003-09-01.
