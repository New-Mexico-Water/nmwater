# Nambe Falls Reservoir

_Generated by `scripts/reservoir_fill.py` from `catalog/reservoirs.yaml`. Edit the registry, not this file._

A small Reclamation dam on the Rio Nambe supplying irrigation water in the Pojoaque Valley. At under 2,000 acre-feet it is by far the smallest reservoir in these reports, and it is run as a seasonal supply that refills each spring rather than as multi-year storage.

## How to use this data

- **A clean headwater snowmelt signal.** Because it refills almost every spring, the timing and size of its spring rise track Sangre de Cristo snowpack on the Rio Nambe directly.
- **Local irrigation demand.** The summer drawdown is irrigation use in the Pojoaque Valley.

## Read before quoting a number

- A few dozen acre-feet of error is several percentage points here.
- The dam registry lists the primary purpose as fish and wildlife, which does not match how the reservoir is operated.

## Capacity history

Reported storage departs from the current table in earlier years because older tables were in force. 4 table eras were found, detected by comparing each year with the previous one over the elevations both occupied. Departure is the median of reported storage minus the current table's storage at the same elevation. Capacities are at today's pool elevations.

Confidence: `current_table` means reported storage reproduces from the current table; `high` means the lake reached the pool elevation during that era, or came within 10 ft; `medium` within 30 ft; `low` farther, or the monotone fit had to move the estimate more than 2%.

Capacity of the full pool fell from 2,167 to 1,663 acre-feet over the record, 23%.
