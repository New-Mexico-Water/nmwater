# Abiquiu Lake

_Generated by `scripts/reservoir_fill.py` from `catalog/reservoirs.yaml`. Edit the registry, not this file._

An Army Corps flood and sediment control dam on the Rio Chama below El Vado. Its conservation pool is used to store San Juan-Chama water for Albuquerque and other contractors under easements, while the much larger flood pool above it catches spring runoff and storm flows and passes them on at safe rates.

## How to use this data

- **Two different signals in one series.** Storage below the top of conservation is mostly banked San Juan-Chama water. Storage above it is flood water being held back, and the `days_flood_storage` column counts when that happened.
- **Spring runoff on the Chama.** Peak inflow and the timing of flood-pool use record how big and how early the Chama snowmelt was.
- **Managed releases.** Release peaks show when the Corps and contractors moved water downstream, including coordinated spring releases.

## Read before quoting a number

- This is a flood-control dam. Percent full is measured against the conservation pool, which is what the dam normally holds. Storage above it is flood water being held back, measured by `days_flood_storage` and `peak_flood_pct`. Dividing storage by the dam's total or design capacity answers a different question and will read very low.
- The top of the conservation pool was raised from 6,220 to 6,230 ft in April 2024 under the City of Albuquerque easement, adding about 44,000 acre-feet. Percentages use the pool capacity the Corps published for each date, so 2024 onward is measured against the larger pool. A rise in percent full across that date is a definition change, not water.
- Design capacity in the dam registry, 1,369,000 acre-feet, is flood capacity to the top of the dam. It is not a meaningful denominator for how full the lake is.
