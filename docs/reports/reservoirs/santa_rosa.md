# Santa Rosa Lake

_Generated by `scripts/reservoir_fill.py` from `catalog/reservoirs.yaml`. Edit the registry, not this file._

An Army Corps flood and sediment control dam on the Pecos, completed in 1979. Besides flood control it holds irrigation water for the Carlsbad Project downstream, up to an entitlement the Corps recalculates each year, which is why its conservation pool rarely approaches the top.

## How to use this data

- **Upper Pecos runoff.** Inflow is the Pecos delivered out of the Sangre de Cristo and the upper basin.
- **Carlsbad Project storage upstream.** Storage below the entitlement is irrigation water held for release to Sumner and Brantley and on to Carlsbad.
- **Flood use.** Storage above the top of conservation is flood water held back.

## Read before quoting a number

- This is a flood-control dam. Percent full is measured against the conservation pool, which is what the dam normally holds. Storage above it is flood water being held back, measured by `days_flood_storage` and `peak_flood_pct`. Dividing storage by the dam's total or design capacity answers a different question and will read very low.
- The Corps publishes Santa Rosa's conservation and flood pool elevations in NGVD29 but its capacity table and its other levels in NAVD88. Those two pools are shifted by +2.11 ft to match, which reproduces the published conservation-pool storage exactly.
- The conservation pool is a physical pool, but the water that may actually be kept there is capped by the annual entitlement, currently about 103,000 acre-feet. Percentages against the full conservation pool will read low by design.
