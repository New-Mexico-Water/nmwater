# Eagle Nest Lake

_Generated by `scripts/reservoir_fill.py` from `catalog/reservoirs.yaml`. Edit the registry, not this file._

A high-elevation reservoir at the head of the Cimarron River, built in 1918 and owned by the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish. It stores water for downstream irrigation and is a major recreation and fishing lake.

## How to use this data

- **Northeastern headwater conditions.** Storage tracks snowmelt from the Moreno Valley and the surrounding peaks.

## Read before quoting a number

- Elevation is reported only from 2013. Storage before then cannot be tied to a capacity table vintage and is measured against the current table.
- Since 2013 the lake has not risen above about 8,181 ft, roughly 50,600 acre-feet or 62% of full pool, though it routinely held 70,000 to 81,000 acre-feet before 2000. Whether that is drought alone or an operating restriction should be confirmed with the owner before drawing conclusions from the recent percentages.

## Capacity history

Reported storage departs from the current table in earlier years because older tables were in force. 2 table eras were found, detected by comparing each year with the previous one over the elevations both occupied. Departure is the median of reported storage minus the current table's storage at the same elevation. Capacities are at today's pool elevations.

| Era | Dates | Elevation range (ft) | Departure (af) | Full pool capacity (af), confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013-2014 table | 2013-10-01 to 2014-12-31 | 8155.7-8160.8 | 363 | 83,232, low |
| current table | 2015-01-01 to 2026-09-23 | 8156.2-8180.8 | 0 | 81,073, current_table |

Confidence: `current_table` means reported storage reproduces from the current table; `high` means the lake reached the pool elevation during that era, or came within 10 ft; `medium` within 30 ft; `low` farther, or the monotone fit had to move the estimate more than 2%.

Capacity of the full pool fell from 83,232 to 81,073 acre-feet over the record, 3%.
