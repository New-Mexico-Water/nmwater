# Water balance of three Rio Grande reaches

Generated by `scripts/water_balance.py` from the archive; do not edit by hand. The method and every series are in `nmwater/derived/water_balance.py`.

## What this is

For a stretch of river, water that comes in must go out, be stored or be lost. This report writes that down for three stretches of the Rio Grande with the gauges and reservoir records the archive holds, one water year at a time, and shows how much water the gauges do **not** explain. That remainder is not an error to be tuned away: it is where the diversions, the evapotranspiration, the groundwater exchange and the ungauged inflow live, and the size of it is the useful number.

```
unaccounted = inflow - outflow - change in storage - evaporation from the reservoirs in the reach
```

All volumes are acre-feet (shown here in thousands). Flows are the gauges' daily mean cfs times 1.983471 acre-feet per cfs-day. A water year runs October to September and is named for the year it ends in.

**Reading the sign.** Positive: measured water went missing (diverted, evaporated or transpired, seeped away, or a gauge reads high). Negative: more water left than was measured arriving (an ungauged tributary, a return flow or spring, or a gauge reads low). A period needs at least 95% of its days in every flow and volume series and storage readings within three days of both ends; otherwise it has no balance, and the table says so rather than extrapolating.

## What would close the gap

- **Diversions and returns.** The Middle Rio Grande Conservancy District's and the Elephant Butte Irrigation District's records. The archive has the MRGCD's diversion gauges only since September 2026 (`usbr_albuq`); the longer record is an access request ([#12](https://github.com/New-Mexico-Water/nmwater/issues/12)). With them, the Middle Rio Grande's positive remainder splits into what was diverted and what the river and its fields consumed.
- **Evapotranspiration.** Satellite ET (OpenET) would put a number on the consumption the diversions feed ([#18](https://github.com/New-Mexico-Water/nmwater/issues/18)).
- **El Paso and below.** The archive's USGS record at El Paso stops in 1982 and the IBWC record starts in April 2026, so the chain cannot be carried past Caballo.

## Caveats

- Provisional data. The recent years use USGS and Reclamation values that may be revised; re-run after a revision.
- A gauge is read as published: no rating shifts, no re-computation. Small flows are noisy and a percentage of a small inflow is large.
- Reclamation's evaporation is its own estimate, not a measurement.
- Copies of one gauge from other publishers (Reclamation's, the Corps') fill days USGS lacks. Reclamation's copy of the Rio Puerco gauge matches USGS exactly on the days both have, and USGS has no record there from November 2021 to September 2023. A copy carries its publisher's own review status.

## Reproduce

```
uv run python scripts/water_balance.py
```

or from Python:

```python
import duckdb
from nmwater.derived import water_balance as wb
con = duckdb.connect('data/duckdb/nmwater.duckdb', read_only=True)
wb.run(con, wb.REACHES['elephant_butte'], by='water_year')
```

Needs a catalog built with `nmwater catalog build`. Sources: USGS National Water Information System (gauge pages linked above) and the Bureau of Reclamation's [HydroData](https://www.usbr.gov/uc/water/hydrodata/) for reservoir storage, evaporation and releases.
